Solar + storage vs microgrid: what the RFP is really buying

The U.S. Department of Energy defines a microgrid as a group of interconnected loads and local energy resources within clearly defined electrical boundaries that acts as a single controllable entity with respect to the grid; it can operate grid-connected or islanded (DOE Office of Electricity, Microgrid Systems). DOE’s Microgrid Overview fact sheet lists the key components as generation resources (such as solar arrays, generators or wind turbines), battery energy storage, and a microgrid control system that coordinates resources, balances load and handles disconnection from and reconnection to the main grid.

  • Solar + storage: panels plus batteries, usually to cut energy and demand charges or provide limited backup. Success is measured in savings and production.
  • Microgrid: adds islanding, a controller and protection design so critical loads (fire stations, shelters, water plants) keep running in an outage. Success is measured in resilience.

Typical scope in a public solar + storage or microgrid RFP

  1. Load and site study: critical loads, hours of backup required, available land or roof.
  2. Design and interconnection: single-line diagram, utility interconnection application and approval, protection and controls design.
  3. Equipment: PV, battery system, inverters and the microgrid controller to a stated specification.
  4. Installation and commissioning: including islanding tests where the project is a microgrid.
  5. Operations, monitoring and maintenance: multi-year O&M or performance guarantees, often priced separately.
  6. Ownership model: agency-owned (EPC or design-build) or third-party-owned (PPA or energy-as-a-service on agency property). The notice states which.

Evaluation criteria to expect

Most are best-value RFPs: price or lifecycle cost, technical approach, experience with comparable solar + storage or microgrid projects, interconnection plan and schedule, warranty and O&M terms, and for public works the bonding, prevailing-wage and local-hire requirements stated in the notice. Straight construction bids are more often lowest responsive bid.

Funding: California Microgrid Incentive Program

According to the CPUC Resiliency and Microgrids page (read November 22, 2026), the Commission authorized the Microgrid Incentive Program in January 2021 with a $200 million budget to fund clean energy microgrids supporting vulnerable communities hit by grid outages. On April 6, 2023 it approved program rules allocating $79.2 million to PG&E, $83.3 million to SCE and $17.5 million to SDG&E; projects selected can receive up to $15 million. The utilities run the application windows, so confirm current status with PG&E, SCE or SDG&E before planning a bid. We have not verified whether any window is open today.

What is a microgrid RFP?

A solicitation to design, build or supply a microgrid: per the U.S. Department of Energy, a group of interconnected loads and local energy resources within clearly defined electrical boundaries that acts as a single controllable entity with respect to the grid and can run grid-connected or islanded. Typical components are generation such as solar, battery energy storage and a microgrid controller.

What is the difference between a solar + storage RFP and a microgrid RFP?

A solar + storage RFP buys a solar array paired with batteries, usually to cut demand charges or provide backup. A microgrid RFP adds the ability to disconnect from the utility grid and keep critical loads running, with a controller and protection scheme designed for islanded operation, so scope and evaluation weight resilience and interconnection much more heavily.

What do public solar + storage RFPs usually require?

Expect a site and load study, a system design, interconnection approval from the utility, equipment to stated standards, installation, commissioning, monitoring, and multi-year operations and maintenance. Public works rules such as prevailing wage and bonding often apply; the notice states which.

Who funds public microgrids?

Funding varies by state and program. In California, the CPUC's Microgrid Incentive Program authorized $200 million in January 2021 for clean community microgrids, allocated $79.2 million to PG&E, $83.3 million to SCE and $17.5 million to SDG&E, with up to $15 million per selected project, according to the CPUC. Check the program administrator for whether a window is open.
