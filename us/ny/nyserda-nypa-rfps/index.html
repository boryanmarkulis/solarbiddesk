NYSERDA vs NYPA: who issues what

  • NYSERDA posts funding opportunities on its Funding Opportunities page (read November 24, 2026). It names four types: the PON (Program Opportunity Notice), the RFP, the Open Enrollment PON for subscription-style incentive programs, and the RFQ (Request for Quotation or Qualifications). It also lists RFIs and RFQLs. For storage, see its Energy Storage Program page.
  • NYPA (New York Power Authority) lists procurement opportunities on its Procurement page and on the NYS Contract Reporter, and says it runs sourcing through the SAP Ariba platform (read November 24, 2026).

How to register and respond

  1. NYSERDA: open the solicitation from the Funding Opportunities page and follow the instructions in the notice. NYSERDA offers update emails there.
  2. NYPA: NYPA says use of SAP Ariba requires free registration; registration questions go to nyparfq@nypa.gov. Direct questions about a specific RFP to the contact named in it.
  3. Both: read the full notice for eligibility, forms and deadline before you start. Dates and terms in this page's table are only a summary.

What is the difference between NYSERDA and NYPA solicitations?

NYSERDA, the state energy research and development authority, publishes funding opportunities as PONs, RFPs, RFQs and open enrollment PONs on its Funding Opportunities page. NYPA, the Power Authority of New York, posts RFPs and bids on its own Procurement page and on the NYS Contract Reporter. We list both side by side with the official notice link.

What do PON, RFP and RFQ mean at NYSERDA?

According to NYSERDA: a Program Opportunity Notice (PON) solicits projects toward the goals of a program area, with multiple awards and cost sharing generally expected; a Request for Proposals (RFP) fits a specific area where the work can be described in a detailed Statement of Work; an Open Enrollment PON suits subscription-style incentive programs; and a Request for Quotation or Qualifications (RFQ) procures contracts based on cost or a contractor's qualifications. Always read the notice for the exact rules.

How do I register to respond to a NYPA RFP?

NYPA says it uses the SAP Ariba sourcing platform, which requires free registration, and that questions about registration go to nyparfq@nypa.gov. NYPA also says to direct questions about each RFP or bid to the contact named in that solicitation. Start from NYPA's Procurement page.

Where does NYSERDA post open solicitations?

On its Funding Opportunities page, which lists current and closed PONs, RFPs, RFIs, RFQs and RFQLs, and you can sign up there for update emails. Many also appear on the NYS Contract Reporter, which is where we pick up the notices listed on this page.

Why do some due dates run to 2029 or 2030?

Some entries are long-running program solicitations rather than one-time bids; the date shown is the end date in the notice. One NYSERDA incentive listing says it runs to its date or until funds are fully committed, whichever is earlier, so those programs can close sooner than the date suggests. Check the official notice before you plan around a date.
