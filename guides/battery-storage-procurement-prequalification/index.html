How public agencies buy battery energy storage

Cities, school districts, water districts and authorities usually reach storage through one of these routes. Which are allowed depends on the agency’s procurement rules and state law, so confirm with counsel (general information, not legal advice).

  • RFP (design-build or EPC): scored technical and price proposals; the usual route for custom or larger projects.
  • RFQ or invitation for bids: used when the specification is fixed and price decides.
  • Prequalification or qualified-vendor list: a first step that screens suppliers or integrators on certifications, finances, safety record and references; only approved firms may then bid or be called upon.
  • Cooperative or piggyback contracts: an agency buys off a contract another public body already competed, if its rules allow.
  • Third-party ownership: a developer owns the system and the agency pays under a services or PPA-style agreement. See our solar PPA RFP guide for the structure on the solar side.

What a supplier prequalification package asks for

  1. System certification: UL 9540 is the safety standard for energy storage systems and equipment (scope summary, SCC, read November 23, 2026).
  2. Fire propagation test data: UL 9540A is a test method, not a certification. It covers cell, module, unit and installation-level testing, and its data supports installation instructions, separation distances and the fire and explosion protection required under NFPA 855 and similar codes. The 6th edition was published March 13, 2026 (UL Standards; UL Solutions overview, read November 23, 2026).
  3. Installation code compliance: NFPA 855 is the Standard for the Installation of Stationary Energy Storage Systems; the edition that applies is the one your local authority adopted. Expect questions on spacing, ventilation, detection, suppression and emergency response planning.
  4. Warranty and degradation: capacity guarantee, throughput limits, term, what voids it, and who stands behind it.
  5. Bankability and financial strength: audited financials, years in business, and for new vendors how warranty obligations are backed.
  6. O&M and monitoring: service model, response times, remote monitoring, spare parts and end-of-life or recycling plan.
  7. Safety record and references: comparable public or commercial projects, incident disclosure, and contactable references.
  8. Bonding, insurance and labor terms: bid or performance bonds, liability limits and any prevailing wage rules. The notice states them.

New York: codes and programs

NYSERDA reports that a state Fire Safety Working Group has improved the New York State Fire Code for energy storage, with updates effective December 31, 2025, and that the State’s goal is 6,000 MW of storage by 2030 (NYSERDA Energy Storage Program, read November 23, 2026). Agency and utility procurements such as NYSERDA, NYPA and CPS Energy in San Antonio run on their own sites with their own rules; check them directly. For Albany and the rest of New York, see our New York energy storage RFPs.

Evaluation criteria checklist

  • Is the system UL 9540 listed or certified, and is UL 9540A data available for the stated edition and level?
  • Does the proposed layout meet the adopted fire code or NFPA 855 edition, as confirmed by the authority having jurisdiction?
  • Does the warranty state a capacity or round-trip efficiency guarantee, and what are its limits?
  • Who performs O&M, and what are the response times?
  • Are references verifiable, including projects of similar size and ownership model?
  • Are bonding, insurance and labor requirements met?
  • Lifecycle cost, not just price: augmentation, availability and end-of-life costs.

FAQ

What is energy storage supplier prequalification?

A screening step in which an agency reviews a battery or integrator's documents before, or instead of, scoring a full proposal. It typically checks product certifications (UL 9540 for the system, UL 9540A test data for fire propagation), compliance with the installation code the jurisdiction adopts (often NFPA 855 or a state fire code based on it), financial strength, project references, safety record, warranty terms, and bonding and insurance. The notice defines the exact requirements, so read it before you assemble a package.

How do public agencies procure battery energy storage?

Common routes are a competitive RFP (design-build or EPC with scored technical and price proposals), an RFQ or invitation for bids when the specification is fixed, a two-step process where a prequalified or qualified-vendor list is created first, a purchase through a cooperative or piggyback contract that another agency already competed, and for third-party-owned systems an energy storage services or PPA-style agreement. Which routes are allowed depends on the agency's procurement rules and state law; confirm with counsel. This is general information, not legal advice.

What do UL 9540 and UL 9540A mean for a bid?

UL 9540 is the standard for energy storage systems and equipment; many solicitations ask for a UL 9540 listed or certified system. UL 9540A is a test method, not a certification: it evaluates thermal runaway and fire propagation at cell, module, unit and installation level, and its data is used to support installation instructions and the fire protection required under codes such as NFPA 855. The 6th edition was published March 13, 2026. Ask the agency which edition and which test levels it expects.

What is NFPA 855?

NFPA 855 is the Standard for the Installation of Stationary Energy Storage Systems. Fire codes adopted by states and cities often reference it, so an RFP may require a design that complies with the edition the local authority having jurisdiction has adopted. Adoption and amendments vary by location.
