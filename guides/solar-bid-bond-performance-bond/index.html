Solar Bid Bonds, Performance Bonds and Payment Bonds on Public Projects

Public solar contracts usually need three surety bonds: a bid bond with your bid, then a performance bond and a payment bond before work starts. Federal construction over $150,000 requires the last two. States set their own thresholds, and the RFP states the amounts. As of November 26, 2026 we track 10 open verified public bids.

Bid bond vs performance bond vs payment bond

  • Bid bond (bid security): promises that if you win you will sign the contract and provide the other bonds. If you walk away, the agency can claim it. Some agencies also accept cash or a certified check.
  • Performance bond: protects the agency if the contractor does not finish the work as specified.
  • Payment bond: protects subcontractors and suppliers if they are not paid. Liens generally cannot attach to public property, so the bond is their main remedy.

A surety issues all three and expects you to repay any claim. Underwriting looks at your finances, work in progress and track record, so line up bonding capacity before you chase larger EPC bids.

What the law requires (federal, California, Texas, New York)

  • Federal: performance and payment bonds are required for construction contracts over $150,000; from $35,000 to $150,000 the contracting officer picks alternative payment protections (FAR 28.102-1). When a federal solicitation asks for a bid guarantee, it must be at least 20% of the bid price, capped at $3 million (FAR 28.101-2).
  • California: a direct contractor on a public works contract over $25,000 must give a payment bond, and the call for bids must say so (Civil Code 9550). School district construction bids must come with bid security: cash, a cashier's or certified check, or a bidder's bond from an admitted surety (Public Contract Code 20111). The board sets the amount; 10% is common.
  • Texas: a governmental entity must require a performance bond on public work contracts over $100,000, and a payment bond over $25,000, or over $50,000 for a municipality (Gov. Code ch. 2253).
  • New York: municipal public work over $35,000 goes to the lowest responsible bidder “furnishing the required security” after sealed bids (General Municipal Law 103). The notice sets the security.

How bonds change your bid

  • Price them in. Bond premiums are a real cost. Ask your surety for a rate before bid day and include it as the RFP requires.
  • Check the form. Many agencies require their own bid bond form. A wrong form can make a bid non-responsive.
  • Watch capacity. A large award can use up your bonding line and block the next bid. Sequence your pipeline.
  • PPA and lease deals differ. When a developer owns the system under a PPA, the agency may ask for other security, such as removal security. Read the term sheet; see the solar PPA RFP guide.

FAQ

What is a bid bond on a solar project?

Bid security that guarantees the selected bidder will sign the contract and furnish the required performance and payment bonds. If the bidder refuses, the agency can claim against it. The notice sets the amount and form.

Bid bond vs performance bond: what is the difference?

A bid bond covers the period from bid to contract signing. A performance bond covers completion of the work after award. A payment bond, usually required alongside it, protects subcontractors and suppliers.

When is a performance bond required on public solar work?

Federal construction contracts over $150,000 require performance and payment bonds (FAR 28.102-1). In Texas a governmental entity must require a performance bond on public work over $100,000 (Gov. Code 2253.021). Other states and agencies set their own rules; the RFP states them.

How much is a bid bond?

The solicitation sets it. Federal bid guarantees must be at least 20% of the bid price, up to $3 million (FAR 28.101-2). California school districts set the amount by board rule, and 10% of the bid is common. General information, not legal advice.
