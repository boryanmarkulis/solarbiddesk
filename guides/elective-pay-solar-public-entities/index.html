Elective Pay (Direct Pay) for Solar and Storage at Public Entities

Elective pay lets cities, schools, states and other tax-exempt owners receive clean energy tax credits as a cash refund from the IRS. For solar, timing now decides everything: solar that began construction after July 4, 2026 must be in service by December 31, 2027. Storage is exempt from that cutoff. How elective pay works for public entities

The IRS treats the elective payment as a tax payment and refunds it, so a public owner can get the full value of an eligible credit (IRS: elective pay and transferability). Tax-exempt and governmental entities, such as state and local governments, Tribes and non-profits, can claim the section 48 and 48E investment credits this way (IRS Publication 6045). The entity must complete IRS pre-filing registration and put the registration number on its return.

For bidders, this changes who owns the project. An agency that can get the credit itself may prefer to own the system (EPC or design-build) instead of signing a PPA.

The 2025 law: solar deadlines and what still qualifies

  • Solar and wind: the 2025 law (P.L. 119-21) ends the 48E and 45Y credits for wind and solar facilities placed in service after December 31, 2027, unless construction began by July 4, 2026 (IRS Notice 2025-42; 26 U.S.C. 48E(e)(4)). Notice 2025-42 makes the physical work test the way to prove that start.
  • Energy storage: the termination does not apply to energy storage technology placed in service at a solar or wind facility (48E(e)(4)(C)). Battery storage stays eligible under 48E, which keeps elective pay useful for public BESS projects.
  • EV chargers: the 30C refueling property credit does not apply to property placed in service after June 30, 2026 (IRS FAQ on the 2025 law).
  • Domestic content: for elective-pay projects of 1 MW or more, the credit can be reduced if domestic content rules are not met, unless an exception applies (IRS).

Sources read November 26, 2026. Rules change, and foreign-entity restrictions also apply. Not tax advice; the agency's tax counsel decides.

What to ask in an RFP that mentions tax credits

  • Who claims the credit? The agency (elective pay), or you or a financier (PPA or lease)?
  • Schedule: for solar, can the project be placed in service by December 31, 2027, or did construction start by July 4, 2026? If neither, price without the credit.
  • Prevailing wage and apprenticeship: needed for the full rate on larger projects; see prevailing wage on public solar.
  • Domestic content records: which certifications must you supply for modules, inverters, racking and batteries?
  • Storage scope: batteries remain credit-eligible after the solar cutoff, which can change the project math.

FAQ

What is elective pay for solar?

Elective pay (also called direct pay) lets eligible tax-exempt and governmental entities treat certain clean energy credits, including the 48 and 48E investment credits, as a tax payment the IRS refunds. Pre-filing registration with the IRS is required.

Can municipalities and school districts get the solar tax credit?

Yes, through elective pay; state and local governments are eligible entities. For solar, the 2025 law ends the credit for facilities placed in service after December 31, 2027 unless construction began by July 4, 2026, so timing decides whether a new project qualifies.

Is battery storage still eligible for elective pay?

Yes. The wind and solar termination in 48E(e)(4) does not apply to energy storage technology, so qualifying storage remains eligible under 48E, subject to the other rules such as domestic content and prevailing wage.

Do EV chargers still qualify for the 30C credit?

Not if placed in service after June 30, 2026, per the IRS FAQ on the 2025 law. Federal grant programs such as NEVI are separate from the tax credit.
