NEVI RFPs and CFI Grants for EV Charging: 2026 Status

NEVI is a $5 billion federal formula program that states spend through their own EV charger solicitations; CFI is a separate $2.5 billion grant program. NEVI paused in 2025 and restarted under August 11, 2025 guidance. CFI has no new funding notice. We track 2 open public EV charging bids as of November 28, 2026.

Where NEVI stands in late 2026

  • NEVI provides $5 billion to states to deploy EV charging, first along designated Alternative Fuel Corridors (FHWA interim final guidance, Aug 11, 2025). That guidance replaced all earlier NEVI guidance and asked states to resubmit deployment plans.
  • The Congressional Research Service reports that FHWA made the final year (FY2026) of NEVI funds available in October 2025 pending plan approval, that most state plans have been approved, and that $1.4 billion of $4.2 billion was obligated as of December 31, 2025 (CRS R48996).
  • The federal share for NEVI projects is 80 percent. States may contract with private entities to buy, install, operate and maintain chargers, and the private entity may pay the non-federal share (FHWA guidance).
  • Technical rules stay in 23 CFR part 680. Our EV charging RFP guide covers the four-port, 150 kW, uptime and OCPP requirements.

How a NEVI RFP reaches contractors

NEVI money flows to the state DOT, not to installers. The state then runs its own competitive solicitation, often called a NEVI RFP or round, for site hosts, operators or turnkey teams. Typical steps:

  1. The state publishes corridor segments or target locations and a scoring method.
  2. Teams (charging operator, site host, installer, utility contact) apply with sites, designs and a cost-share offer.
  3. The state awards and signs agreements; the operator then buys hardware and hires installers, sometimes through its own bid.

So an installer can win NEVI work three ways: join an operator team early, bid the operator's subcontract, or bid city, port and transit charging projects funded from other sources.

CFI grants: status and what to watch

The Charging and Fueling Infrastructure program provides $2.5 billion over five years through Community and Corridor grants. Eligible applicants include states, local governments, MPOs, port authorities and Tribes (FHWA CFI page). FHWA says any future funding notice will appear on Grants.gov and its site, and no estimated date is available. CRS reports $1.8 billion awarded to 147 projects in three rounds, the last announced in January 2025 (CRS).

Past CFI winners are mostly cities, counties and transit agencies. Their construction bids are worth watching as they build out.

Sources read November 26, 2026. Program status changes; check FHWA before relying on it. The federal 30C charger tax credit ended for property placed in service after June 30, 2026 (see our elective pay guide).

FAQ

What is a NEVI RFP?

A state's competitive solicitation that awards National Electric Vehicle Infrastructure formula funds to teams that build and operate fast chargers, mainly along designated Alternative Fuel Corridors. The state DOT writes the rules; federal requirements in 23 CFR 680 apply.

Is NEVI funding still available in 2026?

According to CRS, FHWA made FY2026 NEVI funds available in October 2025 pending approval of revised state plans, and most plans have been approved. Each state decides when to open its next solicitation, so check your state DOT.

Is there a new CFI grant round?

As of November 26, 2026, FHWA's CFI page says no estimated date for a new funding notice is available and to watch Grants.gov. The last CFI awards were announced in January 2025.

Who can apply for NEVI and CFI money?

NEVI funds go to states, which can contract with private entities that may cover the 20% non-federal share. CFI applicants are public bodies such as states, local governments, MPOs, port authorities and Tribes. Installers usually take part as team members or subcontractors.
