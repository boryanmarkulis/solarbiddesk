The 8 sections of a solar RFP, and what each should say

Use this as a drafting checklist if you are an agency, or as a bid/no-bid checklist if you respond to RFPs. It is general guidance, not legal advice; the solicitation controls.

1. Scope and site data

State what is to be built or bought and where: site addresses or parcels, roof or land type, existing electrical service, load profile or utility bills, usable area, and restrictions. Say whether storage or EV charging is bundled. Responders cannot price what the site data does not describe.

2. System sizing

Give target size in kW or MW (DC and AC), an annual kWh or percent-of-load goal, storage power and duration if any, and whether alternates are allowed. Say whether size is a ceiling, a floor or a range.

3. Ownership model: EPC vs PPA vs §4217 / ESPC

  • EPC / design-build: the agency pays to build the system and owns it and its output.
  • PPA: a developer owns and operates the system and the agency buys energy at a stated rate over a long term, usually with a roof or ground lease. See solar PPA RFPs.
  • Statutory energy service contract / ESPC: in California, Gov Code §4217.12 lets a public agency enter an energy service contract and related ground lease if its governing body decides at a regularly scheduled public hearing, noticed at least two weeks ahead, and finds that anticipated energy cost under the contract is less than the anticipated marginal cost of the energy it would otherwise consume. §4217.16 says the agency may request proposals first and may award on experience, type of technology, cost to the agency and other relevant considerations. Other states have their own ESPC statutes. See the California school §4217 guide.

How it looks in published bids:

  • EPC example: Albany GSD Landfill Solar PV Project EPC Services is posted by the Power Authority of New York as an “EPC services” solicitation for a solar PV project, so the buyer owns the project and contracts the build.
  • Site lease example: Land Lease & Development Opportunities for Energy Storage (Power Authority of New York) is titled a land lease and development opportunity for energy storage; the title tells you the buyer is offering land rather than buying a built system.

4. Interconnection

The RFP should say who applies, who pays for utility upgrades and which tariff governs. In California investor-owned utility territory, CPUC Rule 21 governs interconnection of net-metered, non-export and avoided-cost generating and storage facilities; projects selling into FERC wholesale markets use a different tariff. In New York, the Standardized Interconnection Requirements (effective October 1, 2026) cover distributed generation and storage of 5 MW or less connected in parallel with utility distribution systems.

5. Prevailing wage, apprenticeship and bonding

California’s Department of Industrial Relations states that anyone working on a public works project must be paid prevailing wages and that projects of $30,000 or more must meet DIR apprenticeship requirements. New York’s Department of Labor enforces prevailing wage under Articles 8 and 9 of the Labor Law and, from December 31, 2025, requires contractors on Article 8 projects to submit certified payroll electronically through its MPWR portal. Bid, performance and payment bond amounts, licensing and insurance are set in each notice, so list them explicitly.

6. Evaluation criteria

List criteria with weights: price or energy rate, technical approach, experience on comparable public projects, schedule, safety record, and for a PPA the developer’s financing strength. Say whether price is scored or only breaks ties. Under §4217.16 a California agency may weigh experience, technology type and cost.

7. Schedule

Include release date, pre-proposal meeting or site walk, question deadline, addenda policy, due date and time zone, interviews, award and board dates, notice to proceed and target operation date.

Real timelines from the bids above, as published on the agency notice:

  • Albany GSD Landfill Solar PV Project EPC Services (Power Authority of New York, NY): posted 2026-10-02, due Dec 3, 2026, time not published.
  • Land Lease & Development Opportunities for Energy Storage (Power Authority of New York, NY): posted 2026-06-24, due Jun 24, 2028, time not published.
  • Affordable Solar and Storage Predevelopment and Technical Assistance (NYSERDA, NY): posted 2020-06-24, due Dec 31, 2029, time not published.
  • Residential and Retail Energy Storage Market Acceleration Incentives Programs (NYSERDA, NY): posted 2025-04-23, due Dec 31, 2030, 3:00 PM ET (or until funds are fully committed, whichever is earlier).

8. Submission requirements

Say where to submit (agency portal or sealed), format and page limits, required forms, insurance certificates, references, the pricing sheet, how to take exceptions to the contract, and the named contact. Check the notice for addenda: published statuses such as “Addendum Posted” appear on state portals, and a missed addendum can disqualify a response.

FAQ

What sections does a public solar RFP usually include?

Most include scope and site data, system size, the ownership or contract model (EPC, PPA or an energy service contract), interconnection responsibilities, labor requirements such as prevailing wage and bonding, evaluation criteria, a schedule, and submission instructions. Our free outline lists all eight with the questions each one should answer.

What is the difference between an EPC RFP and a PPA RFP?

In an EPC or design-build RFP the agency pays to build the system and owns it. In a PPA RFP a developer owns and operates the system and the agency buys the energy at a stated rate under a long-term contract, often with a roof or ground lease. Each notice states which model it uses.

What is Government Code §4217 and does it apply outside California?

§4217.10-4217.18 is a California statute that lets public agencies, including school and community college districts, enter energy service contracts for solar and other alternate energy at their facilities after a public-hearing finding. It is California law. Other states have their own statutes, often called energy savings performance contracting, so check the state's rules.

Does prevailing wage apply to public solar projects?

Often yes. California's Department of Industrial Relations says anyone working on a public works project must be paid prevailing wages, and that projects of $30,000 or more must meet DIR apprenticeship requirements. New York's Department of Labor enforces prevailing wage under Articles 8 and 9 of the Labor Law. Confirm what the specific notice requires.

Who handles interconnection in a public solar RFP?

The notice should say. Interconnection is governed by the utility's tariff: in California investor-owned utility territory, CPUC Rule 21 covers NEM, non-export and avoided-cost facilities, and in New York the Standardized Interconnection Requirements cover distributed generation and storage of 5 MW or less. Wholesale projects use different tariffs.
