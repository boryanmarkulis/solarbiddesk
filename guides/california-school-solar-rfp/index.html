Gov Code §4217 energy service contracts, explained

Chapter 3.2 of Division 5 of the Government Code (§4217.10–4217.19, “Energy Conservation Contracts”) is the statute behind many California school solar projects. The points below paraphrase the statute text on leginfo.legislature.ca.gov. This is not legal advice, so read the sections and the specific solicitation.

  • Who it covers. §4217.11 defines “public agency” to include a school district and a community college district. It defines an “energy service contract” as a contract under which a person provides electrical or thermal energy or conservation services to the agency from an energy conservation facility, meaning alternate energy equipment (solar is listed), cogeneration equipment or conservation measures in public buildings or on agency land.
  • Purpose. §4217.10 lets public agencies develop energy conservation, cogeneration and alternate energy supply sources at their facilities under the chapter.
  • Hearing and findings for an energy service contract. §4217.12 requires a determination at a regularly scheduled public hearing, with public notice at least two weeks in advance, and findings that the anticipated cost of the energy or conservation services is less than the anticipated marginal cost of the energy that would otherwise be consumed, and that any difference between fair rental value and agreed rent for a related ground lease is anticipated to be offset by below-market energy purchases or other benefits.
  • Financing contracts. §4217.13 covers facility financing contracts and ground leases, with a similar public hearing and a finding that repayment or project costs are projected to be covered by energy sales revenue or by funding that would otherwise have bought energy.
  • Selling energy. §4217.14 allows contracts for the sale of electricity, capacity or thermal energy produced by the facility on terms approved by the governing body.
  • Rate projections. §4217.15 says the agency may, but need not, base its findings on projections from the serving utility, the Public Utilities Commission, the State Energy Commission or the Department of General Services.
  • Requesting proposals. §4217.16 says that before awarding, the agency may request proposals from qualified persons and may award on experience, technology type, cost to the agency and other relevant considerations.
  • Not exclusive. §4217.17 says the chapter does not limit an agency's authority to build energy projects or use other leases and contracts allowed by existing law. §4217.18 directs that the chapter be construed to give agencies the greatest flexibility in structuring agreements.

PPA vs ownership for a school or college

PPA / energy service contract: a developer owns and operates the system on district property (typically under a ground or roof lease) and the district buys the energy, so the district pays little or nothing up front. Term, rate, escalator and end-of-term options are set in each solicitation.

District ownership: the district pays for design and construction, for example through an EPC, design-build or bid process, and owns the system and its output. Which route applies is stated in the notice and the board agenda.

Typical scope and prequalification

School and college solicitations commonly bundle solar canopies or rooftop PV with battery storage and EV chargers on one or several campuses, and may ask for design, installation, financing and long-term operations. Requirements vary by district, so check the RFP for:

  • Registration on the portal named in the notice (PlanetBids, BidNet and similar)
  • Contractor licensing and any prequalification the notice requires
  • Experience on comparable public-agency or school projects, plus references
  • Insurance, bonding and any required forms
  • Mandatory pre-bid meetings or site walks and the question deadline
  • Pricing form: lump sum, or rate and term for a PPA

Always confirm requirements and addenda on the official notice.

FAQ

What is Government Code §4217.10-4217.18?

Chapter 3.2 of Division 5 of the California Government Code (Energy Conservation Contracts). It lets public agencies, a term that §4217.11 defines to include school districts and community college districts, develop energy conservation, cogeneration and alternate energy supply sources at their facilities, using energy service contracts, facility financing contracts and facility ground leases.

What does a California school district have to find before signing an energy service contract?

Under §4217.12, the governing body decides at a regularly scheduled public hearing, with public notice given at least two weeks in advance. It must find that the anticipated cost of the energy or conservation services will be less than the anticipated marginal cost of the energy it would otherwise have consumed, and that any gap between fair rental value and the agreed rent for a related ground lease is expected to be offset by below-market energy purchases or other benefits.

Can a district request proposals under §4217?

Yes. §4217.16 says that before awarding or entering into an agreement or lease, the agency may request proposals from qualified persons, and may award on experience, type of technology, cost to the agency and other relevant considerations.

Does §4217 replace other ways of buying solar?

No. §4217.17 states the chapter does not limit an agency's authority to construct energy conservation projects or to enter other leases or contracts for alternate energy facilities under existing law. Districts also use ordinary RFPs, bids and design-build. Check each notice for the procurement route it names.

What is the difference between a PPA and owning the system?

In a PPA the developer owns and operates the system and the district buys the energy under an energy service contract, usually with a ground or roof lease. With ownership the district pays to build the system, for example under an EPC or design-build contract, and receives the output. Terms are set in each solicitation, so read the notice.
