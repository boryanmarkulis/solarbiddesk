Solar PPA RFPs: How Public Agencies Buy Solar, and Open PPA Solicitations

A solar PPA RFP is a public agency's request for proposals from developers who will design, build, own and operate a solar system on its property and sell the power back under a power purchase agreement. Schools, cities, counties, water districts, universities and state authorities issue them, and the winning bidder is chosen on price per kWh plus qualifications. None are open in our feed today.

How a public-agency solar PPA procurement works

The agency publishes a solicitation on its portal or a state notice board, holds a pre-bid meeting or site walk, takes written questions, and then scores the proposals it receives. The winner signs a PPA (and usually a site or roof lease) with the agency.

RFP vs RFQ

An RFP asks for a full proposal (pricing, technical approach, financing, experience) that is scored on several criteria. An RFQ asks for qualifications first and often shortlists firms before a second pricing round. An IFB, by contrast, awards to the lowest responsive bid and is more common for EPC construction than for PPAs.

Term length and who owns the system

In a PPA the developer, not the agency, owns the system for the contract term and sells the energy at a stated rate, so the agency pays no upfront capital cost. Term, rate escalator, and end-of-term options (renewal, removal or purchase) are set in each solicitation, so read the specific notice rather than assuming a standard.

Typical evaluation criteria

  • Price per kWh and escalator
  • Developer qualifications and comparable public-sector projects
  • Financing capability and project team
  • Technical design, production estimate and schedule
  • Operations, maintenance and performance guarantees
  • Local or small-business participation where the agency requires it

California: Gov Code §4217 energy-services note

In California, agencies can also procure solar and other energy projects under Government Code §4217.10–.18 energy-services contracts, awarded after a public hearing finding that energy cost savings exceed costs, instead of a traditional low-bid process. See California solar RFPs for open CA bids.

What bidders need to submit

  • Registration on the portal named in the notice (PlanetBids, ESBD/CMBL, NYSCR and similar)
  • Proposal narrative and project approach
  • Pricing form (rate, escalator, term, production estimate)
  • Company and team experience, references and financial information
  • Required forms, insurance and bonding statements listed in the RFP
  • Attendance at any mandatory pre-bid meeting and questions submitted by the stated deadline

Always confirm requirements and addenda on the official notice.

FAQ

What is a solar PPA RFP?

A request for proposals in which a public agency asks developers to build, own and operate a solar system on its property and sell the power back at a contracted rate, so the agency usually pays no upfront capital cost.

Who issues solar PPA RFPs?

School districts, cities, counties, water and wastewater districts, universities and state authorities that want on-site or nearby solar without owning the system.

What is the difference between a solar PPA RFP and an EPC bid?

In a PPA the developer owns the system and the agency buys the energy over a long term. In an EPC or design-build bid the agency owns the system and pays a contractor to build it. Check the notice for which structure is used.

How long is a typical public solar PPA term?

Terms are set in each solicitation and are commonly measured in decades, often with renewal or buyout options. Read the specific RFP, since term, escalator and buyout terms vary by agency.

Where do I find open solar PPA RFPs?

On agency portals (for example PlanetBids), state boards such as Texas ESBD and the NY Contract Reporter, and SAM.gov for federal work. SolarBidDesk lists open CA, TX and NY solar bids daily with a link to the official notice.
