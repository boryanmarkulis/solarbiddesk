Step 1: bid or no-bid in one day

Most lost bids were never winnable. Score each notice before anyone opens a design tool.

  • Fit: is the scope solar PV you build (rooftop, carport, ground mount), or an O&M, design-only or land-lease notice?
  • Contract model: EPC (the agency owns), PPA (you own and sell power) or an energy services contract. Each needs a different price sheet.
  • Gates: licenses, bid bond, prevailing wage registration, experience minimums and insurance limits. One missed gate ends the bid.
  • Time: under 10 working days to the due date is tight for a priced design-build response. Mandatory site walks shorten it further.
  • Competition: check the plan-holder list if the portal shows one.

If two or more answers are bad, pass and spend the hours on a better notice.

Step 2: build a compliance matrix from the RFP

Copy every “shall”, “must” and required form into a spreadsheet: requirement, RFP section, where your answer sits, and owner. Evaluators score against their own sheet, so mirror its headings and order. Typical items:

  • Signed forms: bid form, addenda acknowledgment, non-collusion, references, and any local-preference or small-business forms.
  • Bid security, usually a bid bond (see our bid bond and performance bond guide).
  • Wage and labor terms (see prevailing wage on public solar projects).
  • Technical proposal: layout, equipment list, production estimate, interconnection plan, schedule.
  • Price form in the exact format requested, with alternates only where allowed.

Track addenda daily until the deadline. Agencies change dates, quantities and forms late.

Step 3: write to the evaluation criteria

Public RFPs usually publish their criteria and weights. A common mix is price, qualifications and experience, technical approach, schedule and references. Give each criterion its own section with the same title, and lead each section with the answer.

  • Experience: 3 to 5 similar public projects with size in kW, year, owner contact and photos.
  • Technical approach: how you handle roof or site conditions, interconnection, permitting and commissioning.
  • Schedule: a dated plan tied to the notice milestones, including utility lead times.
  • Risk: name the two or three real risks and your fix. Evaluators trust a bidder who sees them.

Step 4: price to the contract model

An EPC bid prices the build. A PPA bid prices energy per kWh over the term, plus escalator and buyout terms; see the solar PPA RFP guide. An energy services bid ties price to guaranteed savings; see ESPC vs PPA. Federal tax credit timing now matters for solar pricing: read our elective pay guide before you assume a credit. State whether your price includes bonds, prevailing wage and utility upgrade costs, because evaluators compare like for like.

Step 5: submit, then debrief

Upload or deliver a day early. Portals slow down at deadline hour, and late bids are usually rejected. After award, ask for the score sheet or a debrief, and request the winning price where public records rules allow. That is the cheapest market data you will get, and it makes the next response sharper.

FAQ

How do I respond to a solar RFP from a public agency?

Decide bid or no-bid quickly, then build a compliance matrix from every required item, write one section per evaluation criterion in the RFP's order, price to the stated contract model (EPC, PPA or energy services), include the bid security and forms, and submit before the deadline. The solicitation controls; read every addendum.

What do evaluators look for in a solar proposal?

Whatever the published criteria say. Many public solar RFPs weigh price, relevant experience, technical approach, schedule and references. Answer each criterion under its own heading with evidence such as comparable projects, production estimates and a dated schedule.

How long do I have to respond to a public solar RFP?

The notice states the due date and time. Mandatory pre-bid meetings and site walks often come early, so check those dates first.
